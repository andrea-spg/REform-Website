Welcome to REform, an open-source hardware Recovery Network engineered to intercept discarded domestic appliances and redirect their fully functional components back into a circular lifecycle. By providing simple, intuitive design blueprints, users are encouraged to transform salvaged components into refurbished and circular household alternatives.

Every year, millions of functional appliances are thrown away due to a single isolated failure, a lack of repair options, or an unfamiliarity with how these everyday products actually function on the inside. REform breaks this cycle. Within our ecosystem, the salvageable mechanical and kinetic components of these products are recovered and reused into entirely new contexts through structured, step-by-step guides tailored to the specific appliance you are approaching.

REform collects all the needed parts, files and local resources you need to navigate this design process in the most sustainable manner possible, accessible in this platform. Additionally, as a user REform also serves as an accessible gateway to understanding the foundations of electronics and mechanics — you will acquire the practical skills needed to unlock the full potential of these salvaged components.

Life Cycle Assessment

Is rebuilding really better than buying new? We ran a screening LCA on the same job — "move air across a desk" — comparing a mass-produced ADE USB desktop fan against the REform Level 1 Ventilator built from a salvaged hair dryer. This focuses on the embodied carbon of the materials (excluding manufacturing and transport), where the numbers are most directly comparable.

A Life Cycle Assessment (LCA) measures a product's environmental footprint across every stage of its life — from raw materials pulled out of the ground to the moment it is thrown away. This is a screening LCA with a gate-to-grave boundary: it compares two products that do the exact same job so the difference comes down to design choices, not function.

The Scenario

Functional unit: one small desktop fan, used 2 hours/day across summer (≈ 90 days/year) for a 4-year service life — about 720 hours of air movement. Both products are measured against this identical usage profile.

Manufacturing

The ADE fan relies on global infrastructure — fossil-fuelled injection moulding, lithium cell synthesis, and international shipping to an EU warehouse. The REform build is fabricated locally with digital tools: roughly 0.75 kWh of 3D printing plus 0.08 kWh of laser cutting — about 0.83 kWh of local grid electricity in total, with no transport leg.

End of Life

What the assessment shows

Swapping fossil-based ABS for bio-based PLA and renewable plywood cuts the housing's embodied carbon by ≈ 55% — from 1.023 down to 0.460 kg CO₂-eq — while nearly halving the total material mass (610 g → 326 g).

On top of that material saving, the build inherits its motor, switch and power path from a discarded hair dryer, so the mechanical core carries zero carbon debt, and it is fabricated locally (≈ 0.83 kWh) with no shipping leg. At end-of-life the motor returns to the parts pool as a donor for the next build rather than becoming shredder feedstock. That inner loop is the whole point.

Scope note: figures cover embodied material carbon (gate boundary). Manufacturing and transport are described qualitatively, not yet quantified for the ADE baseline, so no single combined-lifecycle percentage is claimed.
